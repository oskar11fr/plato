# Summarize a source

Write a page the reader can read in five minutes to understand what the document says and why it matters, and come back to later as a reference.

## Structure

Right under the title, write a **TL;DR** of two or three sentences: what was done and the main result. Then use these sections, leaving out any that don't apply:

- `## Problem`: the question the work addresses and why it matters.
- `## Approach`: the method, model or argument. Give key equations in LaTeX and say what each symbol means.
- `## Results`: the main findings with the actual numbers, and what they were measured on and compared against.
- `## Limitations`: those the authors state, and any that are apparent from the text. Say which is which.
- `## Key concepts`: the concepts a reader needs, one per line, each as a wiki link with a one-line definition.
- `## Open questions`: only if the document raises some worth following up.

For documents that aren't research papers (a book chapter, a report, lecture notes), adapt the sections to the document's shape.

## Accuracy

- State only what the document says. If something is unclear or missing, say so rather than filling the gap from general knowledge. If background knowledge is needed to follow the page, mark it as background.
- Cite the page for every specific claim, number and equation.
- Report numbers exactly as given, with their units and setting.
- Quote the document's exact wording only for definitions and central claims.

## Style

- Plain, direct sentences. Explain terms on first use.
- Aim for 400 to 900 words; go longer only for a long, dense document.
- No filler such as "this paper presents an interesting approach".

## The reader's input

- If the reader names a focus, give that part more depth and keep the rest brief.
- If the reader highlighted passages, make sure the page covers what they say.
